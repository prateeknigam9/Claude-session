## COLD OPEN: It's Not Flat (as approved in the test: `opening-v2/`)

(low) September, twenty twenty. New York.
A small team of researchers has been digging into one company for months.
Old videos. Internal messages. Former employees, finally talking.
And one video they keep coming back to.

(warm) A huge white truck, gliding down a desert road. Silent. Smooth. The future.

(build) The company behind it is worth about thirty billion dollars. More than Ford.
And General Motors has just agreed to buy a two-billion-dollar piece of it.

(low) But the researchers have noticed something about that road. [pause]

(hit) It's not flat. [pause]

(low) According to their sources, the truck wasn't driving at all.
Someone had towed it to the top of a hill… and let it roll.

(build) So now they have a choice.
Publish… and take on one of the hottest companies in America, and the biggest carmaker in the country.
(low) Or stay quiet. [pause]

(hit) On September tenth, they hit publish.
(build) What happened next would end in a courtroom… a bankruptcy… and a phone call from the White House.

This is the paper trail. Case three. The Hill.

## PART 1: THE SHOWMAN

[SCREEN: chapter card. Then: black. A spotlight snaps on. DEC 2016 · SALT LAKE CITY, small, typewritten.]

(low) To understand that video, you have to go back four years. To a night in Salt Lake City.

[SCREEN: a packed hall, from the back row. Paper heads, phone lights. On stage, a huge shape under a grey sheet.]

(warm) December, twenty sixteen. Hundreds of people, a stage, music loud enough to feel in your chest. And under a giant sheet… something big.

[SCREEN: a man walks into the spotlight: the generic founder puppet, open collar, beard, mic in hand.]

(build) The man on stage is Trevor Milton. Thirty-four years old. No engineering background.
But when he talks, people lean in.

[SCREEN: the crowd leans forward, all at once.]

(low) Milton grew up in Utah. He didn't finish college. He'd sold security alarms. He'd run a small company that converted trucks to natural gas.
(warm) And two years earlier, he'd started a new one. He named it after the inventor Nikola Tesla. The same inventor another famous car company is named after.

[SCREEN: quick memory cut-outs pinned behind him in the dark: a small house, an alarm box, a gas-station pump, an old sepia portrait (generic). Each lit for a second, then gone.]

(build) Tonight is the night he shows the world what that company can do.

(warm) He tells them about trucks that run on hydrogen. No diesel. No smoke. Just water dripping from the pipe.
Trucks that will change how the whole world moves things.
And hydrogen stations, from coast to coast, so they'd never run dry.

[SCREEN: the sheet ripples. A drop of water falls in slow motion.]

(build) And then… the sheet comes off.

[SCREEN: the sheet flies. Flashbulbs. The white Nikola One gleams.]

(hit) The Nikola One. Gleaming. Enormous. The crowd goes wild.

[pause]

(low) But according to people who later spoke out, some of those who'd built it knew something the crowd didn't.

[SCREEN: backstage, darker. Two worker silhouettes look at each other. The camera slips under the truck: the bay is empty.]

(low) According to prosecutors, that truck couldn't drive. Not across the stage. Not anywhere.
It was a beautiful shell.

[SCREEN: the truck's side panel lifts away, just for a second: empty. Then it closes. Small tag: PROSECUTORS.]

(build) It didn't seem to matter. The orders started pouring in. Or at least, that's how it sounded.
Thousands of truck reservations. Big companies signing up. A giant brewer asked for hundreds of trucks.

[SCREEN: reservation slips flutter down onto the stage like confetti.]

(low) Critics would later point out that many of those reservations could be cancelled for free. A "maybe", counted as a sale.

[SCREEN: one slip lands face up; the camera reads just one word: REFUNDABLE.]

(warm) About a year later, in early twenty eighteen, Nikola posted a video. The truck, gliding down a desert road.
They called it "In Motion".

[SCREEN: the hill video plays on a phone screen in someone's hand. Likes ticking up.]

(low) You already know about that road.

[pause]

(warm) And around the same time, Trevor Milton was making a deal with a rancher in Utah.

[SCREEN: wide open land, a fence, a farmhouse at dusk. A man in a hat (generic puppet) at the gate. EXAMPLE OF A REAL VICTIM · NOT A LIKENESS.]

(warm) The rancher owned a beautiful piece of land. Milton wanted it.
So they agreed on a price: some cash… and a big block of Nikola shares.

[SCREEN: a handshake over the gate. A deed changes hands, then a stock certificate.]

(low) The rancher believed what he was told about the company. Prosecutors would later say much of it wasn't true.

[SCREEN: the rancher looks at the certificate. The sun goes down behind him.]

(build) But that was a problem for later. Because in twenty twenty… Nikola was about to take off.

## PART 2: THE ROCKET

[SCREEN: chapter card. Then: a living room at night, TV glow, a phone lighting up a face.]

(low) Spring, twenty twenty. The world is stuck at home.

(warm) Millions of people open a trading app for the very first time. Bored, a little scared, looking for something that goes up.

[SCREEN: EXAMPLE · NOT A REAL PERSON. A young delivery driver (generic puppet) on a sofa, uniform still on, phone in hand.]

(warm) Let's follow one of them. He's an example, not a real person. A delivery driver, twenty-something, with ten thousand dollars saved up.
(low) It's money he's been putting away for two years. For a deposit on a small apartment.

[SCREEN: his phone: a balance, $10,000.]

(build) And that June, Nikola goes public. Not the slow way, with years of checks. Through a shortcut called a SPAC.
A company that already has a stock listing, and no business at all. Nikola climbs inside it, and suddenly anyone can buy a piece.

[SCREEN: a long corridor of locked doors (THE NORMAL WAY), and next to it, a small side door that just swings open.]

(warm) The driver sees the video of the truck. He hears the founder on a podcast. It all sounds unstoppable.

[SCREEN: the hill video on his phone, then the founder puppet in a podcast booth, headphones on, talking with his hands.]

(build) And he's not alone. Within days, Nikola's stock more than doubles.
On paper, the company is worth about thirty billion dollars. More than Ford.

[SCREEN: the phone chart shoots up off the screen and into the room. Ford's name, small, sinks on a paper scale.]

(warm) The driver buys in. Near the top.

[SCREEN: his thumb presses BUY. A little confetti burst on the screen.]

[pause]

(low) Trevor Milton is now a billionaire. On paper.

[SCREEN: the founder alone in a big empty office, city lights below. He looks at his own phone and smiles.]

(build) And prosecutors say that's when the stories got bigger. That Nikola was already making its own hydrogen, cheaply. That it built its key parts itself. That its new pickup truck, the Badger, was built from the ground up, by Nikola.
According to prosecutors, none of that was true.

[SCREEN: speech bubbles grow out of the phone, bigger and bigger, until they fill the room. Each one gets a tiny PROSECUTORS: FALSE tag.]

(low) Meanwhile, two thousand miles away in New York, a small research firm has started asking questions.

[SCREEN: the rainy night office from the cold open, earlier: just one researcher, one lamp, a phone to his ear. DRAMATIZATION.]

(warm) They call people who used to work at Nikola. Some won't talk. Some do.
According to their report, they collected text messages, emails, recorded calls, photos from behind the scenes.

[SCREEN: printouts and photos appear on the desk one by one, each pushed into the lamp's circle of light.]

(build) And they start to piece together how one famous video was made.

[SCREEN: the researcher pins the hill video still to the corkboard. A red string runs from it to a question mark.]

(warm) In September, the biggest prize of all. General Motors agrees to take a stake worth two billion dollars, and to help build that pickup.

[SCREEN: a long boardroom table. Two paper hands shake. Champagne corks. GM and NIKOLA as plain type.]

(build) If America's biggest carmaker believes in you… who could doubt you?

[pause]

(low) Two days later, in a small office in New York… someone hits publish.

[SCREEN: we're back in the rainy night office from the cold open. The key goes down. A printer starts.]

## PART 3: THE REPORT

[SCREEN: chapter card. Then: morning. The same office, daylight now. Phones buzzing on the desk.]

(build) The report lands like a bomb. Its title calls the company "an ocean of lies".
It lists dozens of claims it says are false. And right at the top… the truck on the hill.

[SCREEN: printed pages fan across a trading floor. Screens flick from green to red one by one.]

(low) Now, the people who wrote it were short-sellers. They make money when a stock falls. So they're not neutral, and Nikola points that out, loudly.

[SCREEN: a podium; a company spokesperson silhouette. A paper microphone.]

(warm) The company calls the report false and defamatory. An attack by people trying to profit.
And for a few days, it looks like it might hold.

[SCREEN: the stock wobbles. It steadies.]

(low) Then Nikola puts out a statement about the video. And in it, there's one sentence.

[SCREEN: a single sheet of paper on a dark desk. One line typewritten, appearing word by word.]

(low) It says Nikola "never stated its truck was driving under its own propulsion" in that video.

[pause]

(hit) Read that again.

[pause, 1.5 s: silence, the typewriter cursor blinking]

(low) The video was called "In Motion". And the truck was in motion.
It just wasn't the one doing the moving.

[SCREEN: the hill video, frame tilting to show the slope again. A small arrow labelled GRAVITY.]

(build) After that, everything falls fast.
Ten days after the report, Trevor Milton steps down. He says he'll fight the allegations.
General Motors walks away from the stake. The pickup truck is cancelled.

[SCREEN: the empty big office. His chair spins slowly, empty. The champagne glasses from Part 2, now on their sides.]

(low) The driver watches his money melt on his phone. He doesn't sell. He's sure it'll come back.
Everyone online keeps saying it will.

[SCREEN: the delivery driver on the sofa at night, the phone's red light on his face.]

(build) And then the government comes. The stock-market regulator. Then federal prosecutors in New York.
Nikola will end up paying a hundred and twenty-five million dollars to settle, without admitting or denying anything.
And Trevor Milton is charged with fraud.

[SCREEN: footsteps in a marble corridor. A folder slides across a table: U.S. v. MILTON.]

(warm) Not for building a bad truck. Start-ups fail all the time. That isn't a crime.
For telling investors it worked, when, prosecutors said, he knew it didn't.

[SCREEN: two doors in a dark corridor: FAILED, which stays open, and LIED, which locks.]

(low) September, twenty twenty-two. A courtroom in Manhattan.

[SCREEN: the courtroom, quiet, wood and paper. Twelve paper jurors in shadow.]

(build) For weeks, prosecutors walk the jury through what Milton told investors. The podcasts. The posts. The interviews. And the truck on the hill.

[SCREEN: the hill video projected on the courtroom wall, huge, rolling silently above the jurors' heads.]

(warm) His lawyers say he was an optimist, not a fraud. A man who believed his own big dreams.
Even Nikola's own chief executive takes the stand, and describes his old founder as "prone to exaggeration".

[SCREEN: a witness box. A voice-bubble, small: "prone to exaggeration".]

[pause]

(hit) The jury finds him guilty. Securities fraud. Wire fraud.

[SCREEN: the gavel comes down. Nothing else moves.]

(low) So that should be the end of the story.

(build) It isn't.

## PART 4: THE PHONE CALL

[SCREEN: chapter card. Then: the farmhouse again, years later. Paint peeling. The rancher at his kitchen table, a letter in front of him.]

(warm) December, twenty twenty-three. The sentencing.
Reporters in the room say he cried, and spoke for half an hour about his good intentions. Prosecutors said he still wouldn't take responsibility.
Prosecutors had asked for eleven years. The judge gave him four.
(low) But he stays free while he appeals.

[SCREEN: a prison gate, half drawn, that stops… and stays open.]

(low) Back in Utah, the rancher is still waiting.
Prosecutors ask the judge to make Milton pay back more than fifteen million dollars to him alone. And about six hundred and eighty million to the shareholders, people like our driver.

[SCREEN: the rancher's letter. Then the driver's phone, the same number glowing on both: WAITING.]

(build) The judge is still deciding how much Milton owes. When the phone rings.

[SCREEN: a dark room. A phone face-up on a table, buzzing, slowly turning on the wood.]

[pause]

(low) March, twenty twenty-five. Trevor Milton tells the world he's just had a call. From the President of the United States.

[SCREEN: the founder at a window, phone to his ear, morning light. We never see his face clearly.]

(hit) A full and unconditional pardon.

[SCREEN: a pardon document, a gold seal pressed down (generic). The prison gate swings fully open.]

(low) No prison. No fine.
And no court could now order him to pay back the investors. Not the rancher. Not the driver. Not a single dollar.

[SCREEN: the rancher's letter folds itself up. The driver's WAITING screen goes dark.]

[pause]

(warm) Why? Here's what's on the record.
In the months before the election, Milton and his wife gave more than one point eight million dollars to support Trump's campaign.
The President said Milton was targeted for being one of his early supporters.
And Milton says he was framed, and never committed a crime.

[SCREEN: three cards on a dark table, each lit by its own small lamp: a donation slip; a quote card (paraphrased); his statement (his claim).]

(low) A pardon forgives the punishment. It doesn't say the jury was wrong.
You can decide what you think of that.

[SCREEN: the three lamps go out one by one, leaving the gavel from Part 3 lit.]

(build) And Nikola? After Milton left, it did build real trucks. Not many. Some caught fire and were recalled.
The stock that had once been worth more than Ford fell more than ninety-nine percent.
In February twenty twenty-five, five weeks before the pardon, Nikola went bankrupt.

[SCREEN: an empty factory floor. A single truck under a dust sheet. A BANKRUPT notice taped to the door.]

(warm) There's one more twist. Years earlier, Nikola had won a separate case against Milton: about a hundred and sixty-five million dollars, for what his actions cost the company.
That debt was civil. The pardon didn't touch it.
But according to court filings in twenty twenty-five, he still hadn't paid it.

[SCREEN: a bill on the empty factory desk, stamped UNPAID. Dust settles on it.]

(low) And the researchers who exposed the truck on the hill? Their firm shut down in January twenty twenty-five.

[SCREEN: the rainy office from the cold open. The lights switch off one by one. The monitor is the last to go dark.]

[pause]

(low) So here's where everyone ended up.
The company: bankrupt. The researchers: gone.
The rancher: still waiting. The driver: ten thousand dollars, worth almost nothing.

[SCREEN: four quick portraits in the dark: the factory door, the dark office, the farmhouse, the sofa.]

(build) And the man the jury convicted? Free. With a pardon in a frame.

[SCREEN: the founder's silhouette against a bright window.]

[pause]

(warm) If there's one thing to take from this case, it's this.
The driver never saw the truck. Neither did most of the people who bought the stock. They saw a video, a stage, a confident man with a microphone.
When someone tells you their invention works… don't watch the video.
Ask to see it drive.

[SCREEN: the hill video one last time, freezing on the truck mid-roll. The camera pulls back: it's on a phone, in the driver's hand, on the board of the whole case.]

(low) Because that truck never drove down the hill.

(hit) Gravity did.

[SCREEN: cut to black. A single paper truck rolls off the edge of the board.]
