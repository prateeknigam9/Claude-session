# Case No. 03: The Hill (Nikola) — script v1, 2026-11-08

- **Format:** long-form, 16:9, about 13.5 minutes (2,258 narrated words), built in 4 parts.
- **Narration:** simple English at about 3.4 words per second, with a hook every 60–90 s.
- **Status:** SCRIPT, waiting for your approval. Next comes the storyboard with sound, then your approval, then the voice.
- **Legal standing:**
  - Trevor Milton was **convicted** by a jury in October 2022, on 1 count of securities fraud and 2 counts of wire fraud. He was acquitted on 1 other securities fraud count.
  - He was sentenced to 4 years in December 2023, and **pardoned** in March 2025.
  - A pardon forgives the punishment. It is not a finding of innocence. So what the jury decided can be stated as **the jury's finding**.
  - The specific false claims are attributed to **prosecutors**.
  - Milton's side ("I was framed") is given in his own words, attributed.
  - Nikola itself settled with the SEC without admitting or denying wrongdoing.
- **Series order:** Case 02 already promised Nikola as Case 03, so Nikola moves up from slot 05. Nirav Modi becomes Case 04 (teased at the end) and De Beers moves to Case 05.

## COLD OPEN: The Truck on the Hill

[SCREEN: a paper road on a slope, desert hills. Timestamp types on: UTAH · 2017. A big white paper truck sits at the top.]

Somewhere in Utah, on a long, quiet road, a crew tows a big white truck to the top of a hill.

[SCREEN: a paper tow rope. The truck creeps up. A camera on a tripod appears at the bottom.]

They set up a camera at the bottom. Then they let go.

[SCREEN: the truck starts rolling down. Speed lines. The camera "records" with a red dot.]

The truck rolls down the hill. Smooth. Silent. It looks like the future.

[SCREEN: a video frame: "NIKOLA ONE · IN MOTION". A play button.]

Months later, the company posts the video online. It's called "In Motion".

[SCREEN: likes, shares, a headline clipping: "the truck of the future".]

And here is the problem. According to prosecutors, that truck could not drive. It had no working motors. It wasn't driving down the hill. It was just… rolling.

[SCREEN: a cutaway drawing of the truck: the engine bay is EMPTY. Stamp: **NO WORKING MOTORS · PROSECUTORS**.]

Three years later, the company behind that truck was worth around thirty billion dollars. For a moment, it was worth more than Ford.

[SCREEN: counter rolls to $30,000,000,000. A paper scale: NIKOLA vs FORD, Nikola tips it.]

It had sold almost no trucks.

[SCREEN: a sales ledger: TRUCKS SOLD · column almost empty. Red circle.]

The man who built it became a billionaire on paper. Then a jury found him guilty of fraud. Then a judge sentenced him to four years in prison.

[SCREEN: a polaroid puppet: neat beard, open-neck shirt, big smile. Tags slam in one by one: **BILLIONAIRE** · **GUILTY** · **4 YEARS**.]

And then… he never served a single day of it.

[SCREEN: the 4 YEARS tag peels off and flutters away. WHO KNEW? note.]

His name is Trevor Milton. And his company was called Nikola.

So I went through the court records, the company's own statements, and the report that brought it all down. And three questions kept coming back.

How do you build a thirty-billion-dollar company around a truck that couldn't drive?

Who finally looked under the hood?

And how does a man convicted of fraud walk away without prison, and without paying his investors back?

[SCREEN: three numbered cards. Red string snaps: THE PAPER TRAIL · CASE 03.]

This is the paper trail. Case number three. The Hill.

And if you've ever bought a stock because a founder sounded sure of himself, this case is about you.

## PART 1: THE SHOWMAN

[SCREEN: chapter card CASE FILE 1 / 4 · THE SHOWMAN.]

Trevor Milton did not come from the car world.

[SCREEN: a small-town map of Utah. A paper house. Timestamp: 1980s · UTAH.]

He grew up in Utah. He didn't finish college. Before trucks, he ran small businesses. One of them sold security alarms. Another was a natural-gas company for trucks.

[SCREEN: index cards pin up: COLLEGE · DROPPED OUT · ALARMS · GAS TRUCKS.]

What he did have was a gift. He could talk. On a stage, in a podcast, on social media, Trevor Milton could make you see things that weren't there yet.

[SCREEN: the puppet on a stage, mic in hand. Speech bubbles fill the board: "the future", "zero emissions", "game changer".]

In twenty fourteen, he started a new company. He named it after the inventor Nikola Tesla. The same inventor another famous company was named after.

[SCREEN: a NIKOLA card (plain type, no logo). Next to it, a portrait cutout: NIKOLA TESLA · INVENTOR · 1856–1943.]

The plan was huge. Big trucks that run on hydrogen. No diesel. No smoke. Only water coming out of the pipe.

[SCREEN: a paper truck with a hydrogen tank. A little paper water drop drips from the exhaust: H₂O.]

And he didn't just promise the trucks. He promised the fuel. A network of hydrogen stations across America.

[SCREEN: a US map. Station pins pop in, coast to coast. Tag: **PROMISED**.]

In December twenty sixteen, he held a big launch event in Salt Lake City. Lights. Music. A crowd.

[SCREEN: a stage set: spotlights, a paper crowd, a sheet covering a huge shape. Timestamp: DEC 2016 · SALT LAKE CITY.]

Then the sheet came off. There it was. The Nikola One. A giant, shiny, futuristic truck.

[SCREEN: the sheet flies off. The truck gleams. Flashbulbs.]

But according to prosecutors, there was something the crowd didn't know.

[SCREEN: a slow push in on the truck. The board darkens.]

That truck was not a working truck. It couldn't drive itself onto the stage. It didn't have the parts inside to make it move.

[SCREEN: the cutaway again: an EMPTY engine bay, a few paper wires going nowhere. Stamp: **NOT DRIVABLE · PROSECUTORS**.]

And yet, the orders started coming in. Or at least, that's how it sounded.

[SCREEN: a stack of paper RESERVATION slips grows. A counter: "billions in orders".]

Nikola said it had thousands of truck reservations. Billions of dollars' worth. Big companies signed up, including one of the world's biggest beer brewers.

[SCREEN: order slips stamp in. A generic beer crate card, no brand: "a giant brewer · 800 trucks".]

But critics later pointed out that many of those reservations were not real orders. You could cancel them. Some needed no real deposit at all.

[SCREEN: one slip flips over: "REFUNDABLE". Another: "$0 DEPOSIT". Red flag 1 appears top-right: RED FLAGS: 1.]

That's red flag number one. When a company counts "maybe" as a sale.

[SCREEN: RED FLAGS: 1 counter ticks.]

Over the next few years, Trevor Milton became one of the loudest voices in the electric vehicle world. He posted almost every day. He did interviews. He promised more and more.

[SCREEN: a wall of social posts slides past, generic: "Hydrogen at a fraction of the cost", "We build it all in house", "Fully functioning".]

And then, in twenty twenty, he found a way to put Nikola in front of millions of ordinary investors. Without ever selling a single truck.

[SCREEN: the camera pulls back to the board. A folder labelled HOW? lands on it.]

That's where the hill comes in.

## PART 2: THE HILL

[SCREEN: chapter card CASE FILE 2 / 4 · THE HILL.]

Let's go back to that video. The truck, rolling down the road in Utah.

[SCREEN: the IN MOTION video frame again, now pinned as EXHIBIT A.]

The company posted it in January twenty eighteen. Trevor Milton promoted it. People saw a working truck. And by most accounts, that's exactly what they were supposed to see.

[SCREEN: a timestamp: JAN 2018. Likes counter. A paper viewer nods: "it works".]

But here's how the trick worked, according to the evidence later shown in court. The truck was towed to the top of a gentle hill. Then it was released. Gravity did the rest.

[SCREEN: a blueprint diagram: 1 · TOW UP. 2 · RELEASE. 3 · GRAVITY. Arrows, a slope angle.]

And according to a later report, the camera was angled, so the road looked flat.

[SCREEN: the video frame rotates a few degrees. A protractor slides in: "camera angled · road looked flat" · REPORT.]

Think about that. A truck sold as the future of power… with no power.

[SCREEN: a paper battery icon, gauge at EMPTY. A small beat of silence.]

Now, a fake video alone doesn't make a fortune. What made the fortune was what happened next.

[SCREEN: a calendar flips fast to 2020.]

In twenty twenty, Nikola went public. But it didn't do it the normal way.

[SCREEN: a stock exchange card. Timestamp: JUNE 2020.]

Normally, a company that sells shares to the public goes through a long process, with lots of checks. Nikola took a shortcut. It merged with something called a SPAC.

[SCREEN: two paper doors. One long hallway labelled IPO with many checkpoints. One short door labelled SPAC.]

A SPAC is basically an empty company that already has a stock listing. It has money, and no business. It merges with a real company, and that company is suddenly on the stock market.

[SCREEN: an empty paper box labelled SPAC. Nikola's card drops in. The box pops onto a ticker board.]

It's faster. And back then, it came with fewer questions.

[SCREEN: the checkpoint signs on the IPO hallway fall over. RED FLAGS: 2.]

That's red flag number two. A company in a hurry to sell you its shares.

[SCREEN: RED FLAGS: 2.]

And in June twenty twenty, ordinary people were buying shares like never before. Many were stuck at home. Many had trading apps on their phones for the first time.

[SCREEN: paper phones with green arrows. A paper sofa. Timestamp: 2020 · LOCKDOWN.]

Trevor Milton spoke to them directly. On podcasts, on TV, on social media. And prosecutors said he told them things that were simply not true.

[SCREEN: a court-style list card: WHAT PROSECUTORS SAID WAS FALSE. Items tick in one by one.]

That the Nikola One was a fully working truck.

That Nikola was already making hydrogen, at a fraction of the normal cost.

That Nikola built its key parts itself.

And that its new pickup truck, the Badger, was built from the ground up and fully working.

[SCREEN: four lines on the list, each gets a red stamp: **FALSE · PROSECUTORS**.]

According to prosecutors, none of that was true.

[SCREEN: a held beat. Camera slowly closes on the list.]

But the market loved it. Within days, Nikola's stock shot up. At one point it nearly tripled in under a week.

[SCREEN: a stock chart rockets up, paper line tearing through the top of the card. Timestamp: JUNE 9 2020.]

On paper, Nikola was worth about thirty billion dollars. More than Ford. A company that had built cars for over a hundred years.

[SCREEN: the NIKOLA vs FORD scale again. FORD · SINCE 1903. NIKOLA · TRUCKS SOLD: ~0.]

And Trevor Milton owned a big piece of it. Tens of millions of shares. On paper, he was now a billionaire.

[SCREEN: the polaroid puppet. A paper crown lands on his head. Tag: **BILLIONAIRE · ON PAPER**.]

He was on top of the world.

[SCREEN: paper confetti, a magazine-style clipping: "the next Elon?".]

And in September twenty twenty, he landed the biggest deal of all.

[SCREEN: timestamp: SEPT 8 2020.]

General Motors, one of America's biggest carmakers, agreed to take a stake in Nikola worth two billion dollars, and to help build the Badger.

[SCREEN: a GM card (plain type, no logo) slides in. A handshake cutout. $2,000,000,000 · 11% STAKE.]

It looked like proof. If the giants believe in you, who could doubt you?

[SCREEN: a gold star sticker on the Nikola card: "VERIFIED?" with a question mark.]

Somebody could.

[SCREEN: a shadow falls across the board.]

Two days later, a small research firm in New York published a report. And its title was brutal.

[SCREEN: a document thuds onto the board, face down.]

## PART 3: THE REPORT

[SCREEN: chapter card CASE FILE 3 / 4 · THE REPORT.]

The firm was called Hindenburg Research. It was a short-seller.

[SCREEN: the document flips: HINDENBURG RESEARCH · SEPT 10 2020.]

Here's what that means. Most investors win when a stock goes up. A short-seller wins when a stock goes down. So a short-seller has every reason to dig for bad news.

[SCREEN: two arrows: UP = most investors win. DOWN = short-seller wins. A shovel cutout.]

That makes them biased. But it also makes them the only people in the room who are paid to ask hard questions.

[SCREEN: a magnifying glass slides over the Nikola card.]

The title of the report was: "How to Parlay an Ocean of Lies into a Partnership with the Largest Auto O.E.M. in America".

[SCREEN: the title typed out letter by letter. "OCEAN OF LIES" gets highlighted in red.]

The report said Nikola had been built on dozens of false claims. And right at the top was the truck on the hill.

[SCREEN: the IN MOTION frame pinned under the report. A red string between them.]

It said the truck had been rolled down a slope, and filmed to look like it was driving.

[SCREEN: the blueprint: TOW UP · RELEASE · GRAVITY. Tag: **HINDENBURG REPORT**.]

Now, a report from a short-seller is not proof. So what did Nikola say?

[SCREEN: a paper envelope: NIKOLA'S RESPONSE.]

Nikola called the report false and misleading. It called it a hit job by people trying to make money off its falling stock.

[SCREEN: the response card: "false", "defamatory", "market manipulation" (paraphrased).]

But then, a few days later, Nikola said something remarkable about the video.

[SCREEN: a slow push in on the envelope.]

It said it had never stated the truck was driving under its own power in that video.

[SCREEN: the quote card, typewriter font: "never stated its truck was driving under its own propulsion". Tag: NIKOLA, SEPT 2020.]

Read that again.

[SCREEN: hold on the quote. Silence under the music. Quote underlined, word by word.]

The company had called the video "In Motion". And the truck was in motion. It just wasn't moving on its own.

[SCREEN: the word MOTION circled. An arrow points down the slope: GRAVITY.]

That's red flag number three. When the defence is about the exact words, not the truth.

[SCREEN: RED FLAGS: 3.]

The stock fell. Fast.

[SCREEN: the chart from Part 2 tumbles down like a paper ladder.]

Ten days after the report, Trevor Milton stepped down as chairman. He said the focus should be on the company, not on him. And he said he would fight the allegations.

[SCREEN: timestamp: SEPT 20 2020. The puppet's crown falls off. Tag: **RESIGNED**.]

Then General Motors walked back its deal. No stake. No Badger. Just a much smaller agreement to supply parts.

[SCREEN: the GM handshake cutout tears in half. $2,000,000,000 is crossed out. Tag: **DEAL SCRAPPED · NOV 2020**.]

The Badger, the pickup truck that was supposed to be fully working, was cancelled.

[SCREEN: the Badger card stamped: **CANCELLED**.]

And then the government stepped in.

[SCREEN: a paper SEC folder, then a DOJ folder, slam down.]

The stock market regulator investigated. And in December twenty twenty-one, Nikola agreed to pay one hundred and twenty-five million dollars to settle the case. It didn't admit wrongdoing, and it didn't deny it.

[SCREEN: a cheque card: $125,000,000 · SEC SETTLEMENT · DEC 2021. Small tag: "neither admitted nor denied".]

As for Trevor Milton, federal prosecutors in New York charged him with fraud. Not for building a bad truck. For lying to investors about it.

[SCREEN: a charge sheet card: U.S. v. MILTON · SECURITIES FRAUD · WIRE FRAUD. Timestamp: JULY 2021.]

That difference matters. Startups fail all the time. That's not a crime. Telling people a truck works when you know it doesn't, so they'll buy your stock… that's what the case was about.

[SCREEN: a split card: FAILING = not a crime. LYING TO INVESTORS = the charge.]

In October twenty twenty-two, the trial ended in Manhattan. Milton's lawyers said he was an optimist, not a fraud. That he believed in what he was saying.

[SCREEN: a courtroom set. The puppet at the defence table. A speech card: "an optimist, not a fraud" (paraphrased).]

The jury didn't buy it.

[SCREEN: the gavel strikes.]

They found Trevor Milton guilty of one count of securities fraud and two counts of wire fraud.

[SCREEN: banner: **GUILTY · 3 COUNTS · OCT 14 2022**. A small tag: "acquitted on 1 count".]

A man who had been worth billions on paper was now a convicted fraudster.

[SCREEN: the polaroid with GUILTY stamped across. Camera pulls back to the whole board.]

But this story has one more turn. And it's the one that made people angry all over again.

## PART 4: THE PARDON

[SCREEN: chapter card CASE FILE 4 / 4 · THE PARDON.]

In December twenty twenty-three, the judge sentenced Trevor Milton to four years in prison and a one million dollar fine.

[SCREEN: a sentence card: 4 YEARS · $1,000,000 FINE · DEC 2023.]

Prosecutors had asked for eleven years. Milton asked for probation.

[SCREEN: a balance scale: PROSECUTORS · 11 YEARS on one side, MILTON · PROBATION on the other. It settles at 4.]

But he didn't go to prison right away. He stayed free while he appealed.

[SCREEN: a paper door, left open. Tag: **FREE PENDING APPEAL**.]

Meanwhile, the people who had lost money were still waiting.

[SCREEN: a paper queue of generic investors with empty wallets.]

Prosecutors asked the judge to order Milton to pay back investors. The amount they asked for? About six hundred and eighty million dollars.

[SCREEN: a counter rolls to $680,000,000. Tag: **RESTITUTION REQUESTED**.]

And one more victim. A Utah rancher, who had sold Milton his ranch in return for cash and Nikola stock. Prosecutors said Milton lied to him too. They asked for more than fifteen million dollars for him alone.

[SCREEN: a ranch cutout, a deed card: "paid in part with Nikola stock". Tag: $15,200,000 · ONE VICTIM.]

The judge never got to decide.

[SCREEN: a held beat. The counter freezes.]

In late March twenty twenty-five, Trevor Milton posted online that he had just received a phone call. From the President of the United States.

[SCREEN: a paper phone rings. A social post card: "full and unconditional pardon" (Milton, Mar 2025).]

President Trump had given him a full and unconditional pardon.

[SCREEN: a PARDON document slides in, with a gold seal (generic, no real seal). Timestamp: MARCH 2025.]

No prison. No fine. And because of the pardon, the judge could no longer order him to pay back a single dollar.

[SCREEN: the 4 YEARS card, the $1M card and the $680M card get crossed out one by one: VOID · VOID · VOID.]

The six hundred and eighty million dollars was gone. Not paid. Just… gone.

[SCREEN: the $680,000,000 counter drops to $0. The investor queue is still standing there.]

So why? Here is what's on the record.

[SCREEN: an evidence table with three cards.]

Federal records show that in the months before the election, Milton and his wife had given more than one point eight million dollars to support Trump's campaign.

[SCREEN: a donation slip: $1,800,000+ · 2024 · FEDERAL RECORDS.]

President Trump said Milton was targeted because he was one of his early supporters.

[SCREEN: a quote card: "targeted for supporting me" (paraphrased), PRESIDENT TRUMP, 2025.]

And Milton says he was framed. That he never committed a crime. That the government went after him for his politics.

[SCREEN: Milton's card: "I was framed" (his claim). A separate tag: THE JURY FOUND: GUILTY · 2022.]

A pardon forgives the punishment. It doesn't say the jury was wrong. And the jury heard the evidence.

[SCREEN: the two cards side by side, connected by a red string with a question mark.]

You can decide for yourself what you think of that.

[SCREEN: the board, quiet. RED FLAGS: 3 stays lit.]

And what happened to Nikola?

[SCREEN: the Nikola card, slightly torn.]

After Milton left, the company did actually build trucks. Real ones. Battery trucks, and later hydrogen trucks. Not many. But real.

[SCREEN: a small row of paper trucks rolls out. Tag: **REAL TRUCKS · 2022–2024**.]

But the battery trucks had problems. In twenty twenty-three, after a fire, Nikola recalled every battery truck it had sold.

[SCREEN: a paper flame cutout. A RECALL stamp: 209 TRUCKS. Timestamp: AUG 2023.]

The company burned through cash. From its peak, the stock fell more than ninety-nine percent.

[SCREEN: the stock chart, now a long slide down. A paper penny at the bottom.]

In February twenty twenty-five, Nikola filed for bankruptcy.

[SCREEN: a BANKRUPT stamp. Timestamp: FEB 19 2025.]

Five weeks later, its founder was pardoned.

[SCREEN: two cards side by side: NIKOLA · BANKRUPT · FEB 2025. MILTON · PARDONED · MAR 2025. A long silence.]

And here's the strangest twist of all. Nikola had won an arbitration case against Milton, worth about a hundred and sixty-five million dollars, to cover what his actions had cost the company. That award was civil, not criminal. The pardon didn't wipe it out. But according to court filings in twenty twenty-five, he still hadn't paid it.

[SCREEN: an arbitration card: $165,000,000 · NIKOLA vs MILTON. Tag: **NOT COVERED BY THE PARDON**.]

So the company he founded went bankrupt, still trying to collect from the man who founded it.

[SCREEN: a red string from the bankrupt Nikola card to Milton's polaroid, pulled tight.]

And Hindenburg, the firm that exposed the truck on the hill? It shut down in January twenty twenty-five. Its founder said he wanted to step away.

[SCREEN: the Hindenburg report card, with a small tag: CLOSED · JAN 2025.]

So by spring twenty twenty-five: the company was bankrupt. The firm that exposed it was gone. And the man the jury convicted was free.

[SCREEN: three cards in a row: BANKRUPT · CLOSED · PARDONED.]

So let's talk about the people this case was really about. The investors.

[SCREEN: EXAMPLE · NOT A REAL PERSON tag. A generic puppet: a young delivery driver.]

Here's an example. Not a real person. A delivery driver in twenty twenty, stuck at home, with a trading app and ten thousand dollars of savings.

[SCREEN: a phone with a trading app. $10,000 · SAVINGS · 2020.]

He watches a video of a truck that seems to drive itself. He hears the founder say it all works. He buys near the top.

[SCREEN: the IN MOTION frame on his phone. A BUY button pressed. Price tag near the peak.]

By the time Nikola went bankrupt, those ten thousand dollars were worth almost nothing.

[SCREEN: the $10,000 shrinks, digit by digit, to a few dollars.]

And the restitution that might have paid some of it back? Wiped out with a phone call.

[SCREEN: the VOID stamp lands on his restitution slip.]

So how do you spot a company like this before it's too late?

[SCREEN: HOW TO SPOT IT card. A checklist.]

One. Count sales, not "reservations". If people can cancel for free, it's not an order.

Two. Be careful when a company races onto the stock market through a shortcut, with fewer checks.

Three. Ask to see it work. Not a video. Not a stage. Independent proof.

And four. When a founder's answer is about the exact words, not the facts, that's your answer.

[SCREEN: the checklist ticks one by one. RED FLAGS: 4.]

[SCREEN: the camera pulls all the way back. The whole board: the truck on the hill, the In Motion frame, the Hindenburg report, the GUILTY banner, the PARDON document, the bankrupt Nikola card, the delivery driver's phone. All joined by red string.]

The truck never drove down that hill. Gravity did.

And for a while, so did the stock. Up was just as fake as the road was flat.

[SCREEN: final card: IT ROLLED.]
